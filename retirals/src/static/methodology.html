    Core Methodology

    1Getting Started▾
    What it means

    Getting started is about entering a realistic picture of your finances today so the planner can project them forward.

    How the planner handles it

    Enter your current age, retirement age, life expectancy, current corpus, annual expenses, annual contribution, return assumptions, and inflation. Add one-time expenses for known future costs such as education, housing, or medical care.

    The default inputs show a realistic mid-career scenario: age 35, planning to retire at 60, with ₹15 lakh annual expenses, ₹40 lakh current corpus, ₹5 lakh annual contribution growing at 8%, and a 60/30/10 equity-debt-arbitrage allocation. This is a plausible starting point for many Indian professionals, but it is intentionally set slightly below full readiness so you can see how the tool identifies gaps and recommends actions.

    Amounts are treated as annual values in Indian rupees. Check that all assumptions use the same time frame and that future expenses are expressed in today's money when entering them.

    The planner also supports a retirement lump sum, pension income, investment allocation, withdrawal tax assumptions, and market-stress scenarios. Use the deterministic simulation for one assumed return path; use Monte Carlo to assess many possible market paths.

    Amounts are in today's money unless stated otherwise. Enter future one-time expenses as the cost today — the planner adjusts them for inflation.

    3Money & Inflation▾
    Amounts are in today's money unless stated otherwise. Enter your expenses and future costs as their value today; the planner adjusts them over time using inflation.
    What it means

    Inflation is the annual increase in the cost of goods and services. An expense of ₹10 lakh today will cost more in the future — for example, at 6% inflation it becomes approximately ₹13.4 lakh after 5 years.

    How the planner handles it

    Your recurring expenses are inflated each year in retirement at the average inflation rate you enter. Contributions stop at retirement; expenses continue to be inflated for as long as the plan runs.

    Future one-time expenses (ad-hoc expenses)

    Enter the cost today. Future cost is adjusted for inflation.

    Each ad-hoc expense is defined by the age at which it occurs, its cost today, and its category. The percentage you associate with a future one-time expense represents its annual inflation rate — the rate at which that cost is inflated from today until the year it is paid (8% is the suggested default for healthcare and education, 5–6% for most other categories). You may use the general inflation rate or a custom rate.

    This is the only place a custom inflation rate applies. Recurring expenses always use the single average inflation rate entered on the main form.

    4Investment Returns▾
    What it means

    Your portfolio return is the growth your savings earn each year. Because your money is split across asset classes, the overall return depends on each part's expected return and its share of the portfolio.

    How the planner handles it

    The planner uses asset-level returns (equity, debt, arbitrage, and REIT) weighted by your allocation, rather than a single fixed return. The pre/post-retirement return fields are kept only for compatibility. In the deterministic projection the same expected returns apply each year; in Monte Carlo the returns vary by simulation.

    Asset return assumptions

    Asset classDefault returnRole in the plan
    Equity12.0%Growth engine of the portfolio.
    Debt6.0%Stability and lower volatility.
    Arbitrage8.0%Moderate return with low sensitivity to equity markets.
    REIT8.0%Real-estate exposure; defaults to 0% allocation.

    Real vs nominal returns

    Use nominal returns when possible. For example, if you expect 10% nominal returns and 6% inflation, your real return is roughly 4%. The planner inflates expenses automatically, so nominal returns are usually easier to reason about.

    Choosing a return rate

    Use a return rate you believe is realistic for your portfolio mix over the long run. Historical Indian equity returns are around 10–12% nominal, but future returns may be lower. A conservative blended assumption of 8–10% pre-retirement and 7–9% post-retirement is common. The planner's default is 10% pre and 8% post.

    5Retirement Projection▾
    What it means

    The deterministic projection traces one expected-case path for your money, based on the fixed assumptions you entered. It shows the mechanics of how your corpus grows before retirement and depletes during it.

    How the planner handles it

    Each year the planner applies the selected asset-level returns, adds contributions before retirement, inflates expenses, applies pension income and withdrawal taxes (including REIT taxation), then updates the corpus. Expenses are treated as an annuity due because they are incurred throughout the year.

    This path is useful for understanding cash-flow mechanics, but it does not show the range of possible market outcomes. Use the deterministic projection for one assumed return path; use Monte Carlo to assess many possible market paths.

    Stress scenarios

    Mild and severe stress scenarios replace the first two retirement-year returns with fixed negative returns. They are designed to show the effect of a poor start to retirement.

    Pension and tax treatment

    Net pension first funds recurring expenses. A pension surplus is reinvested only when that option is selected. Withdrawal taxes use a simplified blended allocation approach and an annual LTCG exemption; they do not track individual tax lots or cost basis. REIT withdrawals are partially taxable: only the estimated gain portion (based on the REIT taxable gain fraction) is treated as taxable and taxed at the LTCG rate.

    6Taxes▾
    What it means

    Your returns and withdrawals are not fully yours — tax reduces what you can actually spend. The planner estimates this so your retirement income requirements reflect it.

    How the planner handles it

    The planner estimates tax each year by blending your asset-class tax rates according to your allocation, and applies the annual LTCG exemption. This is a simplification — it does not track individual cost basis or tax lots — but it is sufficient for projection purposes.

    How rates are blended

    The portfolio's withdrawal tax is a weighted average of the LTCG, STCG, debt, arbitrage, and REIT tax rates, based on your allocation and your equity LTCG/STCG split. REIT taxable gains are taxed at the LTCG rate.

    What LTCG and STCG mean

    • LTCG = Long-Term Capital Gains, taxed at 12.5% for Indian equities above the ₹1.25 lakh exemption.
    • STCG = Short-Term Capital Gains, taxed at 20% for Indian equities.
    • Debt fund gains are taxed as per your income slab (after 3 years).

    The planner blends these based on your equity/debt/arbitrage allocation and equity LTCG/STCG split. It assumes an annual LTCG exemption; it does not track individual tax lots or cost basis.

    8Stochastic Simulation (Monte Carlo)▾
    What it means

    Monte Carlo runs many simulated return paths instead of one. Each path follows the same contributions, expenses, pension, tax, and withdrawal rules, but its market returns differ. The success rate is the share of paths that fund all required expenses through life expectancy.

    How the planner handles it

    The simulation uses either a normal or lognormal return distribution. Lognormal is the default because it cannot produce a return below −100%. A random seed makes a run reproducible; leaving it blank produces a new random set of paths.

    Monte Carlo parameters

    Return distributionLognormalLognormal keeps returns above −100%. Normal is simpler but can produce unrealistic negative portfolio values at high volatility.
    Equity volatility18.0%Annual standard deviation of equity returns.
    Debt volatility6.0%Annual standard deviation of debt returns.
    Arbitrage volatility8.0%Annual standard deviation of arbitrage returns.
    REIT volatility15.0%Annual standard deviation of REIT returns.
    Equity ↔ Debt correlation-0.10How equity and debt returns move together. Negative values mean they tend to offset each other.
    Equity ↔ Arbitrage correlation0.05How equity and arbitrage returns move together. Near-zero values mean they are largely independent.
    Debt ↔ Arbitrage correlation0.20How debt and arbitrage returns move together. Positive values mean they tend to rise and fall together.
    Equity ↔ REIT correlation0.60How equity and REIT returns move together.
    Debt ↔ REIT correlation0.20How debt and REIT returns move together.

    Allocation and volatility

    Equity, debt, arbitrage, and REIT weights, volatilities, and correlations are combined into one covariance-based portfolio volatility. The current model draws one return for the total portfolio; it does not simulate separate asset-class balances or rebalancing.

    9Results & Charts▾
    What it means

    The results translate your inputs and assumptions into a small set of key numbers and charts that tell you how your plan is likely to perform.

    How the planner handles it

    Deterministic results give a single expected-case path and coverage indicators. Monte Carlo results give probabilities and percentile ranges across many simulated paths. Each metric below is described in plain English.

    Key metrics

    MetricMeaning
    Retirement readinessProjected corpus at retirement divided by the estimated minimum corpus required. It is a deterministic coverage indicator, not a probability.
    Probability of successPercentage of Monte Carlo paths with no unfunded expenses through life expectancy. Choose an acceptable target based on your risk tolerance and flexibility; no single threshold fits everyone.
    Median final corpusThe middle final outcome: half of paths finish above it and half below it.
    5th and 95th percentilesRespectively difficult and favourable outcomes. The 5th percentile is exceeded by 95% of paths; only 5% exceed the 95th percentile.
    Funding probability by ageThe chance that expenses are fully funded in a particular year. It helps show the timing of failure risk.
    Failure-age percentilesFor paths that fail, these show when the first unfunded expense occurs. They do not describe paths that remain funded.
    RecommendationsRule-based prompts generated from the results. Treat them as ideas to investigate, not personalised advice.

    Interpreting the numbers

    • Readiness below 100% means your projected corpus is below the deterministic minimum required. It does not mean retirement is impossible — it means the plan is fragile and likely to fail under adverse markets. Use Monte Carlo to see the actual success probability and identify specific actions to close the gap.
    • Good success rate — there is no universal threshold. Many planners target 80–90% for a moderate risk tolerance and 90–95% for a conservative one. If your success rate is below 70%, the plan is fragile; below 50%, it is high risk.
    10Advisory / Strategies▾
    What it means

    The Advisory section helps you see, at a glance, whether your plan is on track and what you could adjust. It is guidance to investigate, not guaranteed financial advice.

    How the planner handles it

    Based on your projected situation, the planner produces a readiness score and solves for the required returns needed to close any gap. It also generates rule-based recommendations (for example, adjusting contributions, expenses, or retirement timing) that you can test by re-running the analysis.

    Not financial advice. The advisory output identifies potential strategies and actions that may improve your retirement outcome based on your projected situation. It is not a guarantee and does not replace review with a qualified professional.

    Advisory outputs

    • Retirement readiness score — a deterministic coverage indicator of projected corpus versus minimum required.
    • Required return for 100% — the pre- and post-retirement return rates needed to reach 100% readiness (goal-seek analysis).
    • Target annual contribution — the additional annual contribution that would close the retirement gap.
    • Recommendations — rule-based prompts generated from the results. Treat them as ideas to investigate, not personalised advice.
    11Limitations & Disclaimer▾
    What it means

    This is a planning and education tool. Projections depend on the assumptions you enter, and actual outcomes can differ from any single projection or simulation.

    How the planner handles it

    The model is a simplified, fixed-assumption projection. It does not represent every real-world behaviour, and it does not provide personalised financial, tax, or investment advice.

    • Returns are independently sampled each year; the model does not represent momentum, mean reversion, or changing market regimes.
    • Inflation, pension growth, and tax rates remain fixed at the values entered.
    • Monte Carlo paths model sequence-of-returns risk because withdrawals continue while return order varies.
    • Asset-class paths, rebalancing, changing allocations, individual tax lots, and changes in spending or health costs are not separately modelled.
    • Correlation inputs must be realistic as a group. Some individually valid combinations are not mathematically valid together and can understate calculated risk.
    • More simulations reduce random sampling noise but do not make uncertain assumptions certain.

    This planner is designed for Indian investors and reflects common asset classes, tax rules, and retirement income sources. It does not provide personalised financial, tax, or investment advice. Always review major decisions with a qualified professional.

    AI Insights▾

    After running both a Predictive Analysis and a Monte Carlo simulation, you can generate an AI-powered interpretation of your results. The AI acts as an interpreter of the engine's outputs — it does not recalculate your plan or invent new numbers.

    How to use

    Run a Predictive Analysis, then run a Monte Carlo simulation. Once both results are available, the ✨ Get AI Insight button becomes enabled. Click it to send your inputs and analysis summaries to the AI provider for interpretation. The insight opens in a new browser tab, so you can keep the planner results visible alongside the AI analysis.

    What the AI does

    • Interprets the deterministic projection and Monte Carlo results in plain language.
    • Distinguishes between engine facts and interpretation.
    • Explains pension, ad-hoc expenses, one-time income, and cash-flow implications.
    • Identifies primary and secondary risks based on the supplied data.
    • Provides evidence-based actions grounded in the actual results.

    Data and privacy

    Your inputs and calculation results are sent to the configured AI provider (Groq or Gemini) for analysis. The AI response is stored temporarily in your browser's local storage and displayed on a separate AI Insights tab. No data is stored on the server beyond the normal API request logs. Numerical recommendations are only provided when explicitly present in the engine's output; otherwise, the AI offers qualitative guidance only.

    Limitations

    • The AI does not provide guaranteed financial advice or product recommendations.
    • All numerical values are preserved exactly as produced by the engine; no rounding or recalculation occurs.
    • The AI respects Indian currency units (rupees, lakhs, crores) and does not invent intervention amounts.
    • If the AI provider is unavailable, the request will fail gracefully without affecting your calculation results.

    Best practices▾
    • Use real returns when possible: If you expect 10% nominal returns and 6% inflation, your real return is roughly 4%. The planner inflates expenses automatically, so nominal returns are usually easier to reason about.
    • Test stress scenarios early: Run Normal, Mild Crash, and Severe Crash before locking in your plan. If a mild crash breaks your plan, you are running too close to the edge.
    • Keep simulations realistic: 1,000–5,000 simulations are usually enough for stable results. More simulations reduce noise but do not remove model uncertainty.
    • Update annually: Re-run the planner every year with actual values. Assumptions drift, salaries change, and markets move.
    • Separate known and unknown expenses: Use ad-hoc expenses for known future costs (education, house, medical) and keep regular expenses in the main expense field.
    • Check the funding probability curve: The final success rate is a summary. The year-by-year funding probability shows exactly when your plan becomes fragile.
    • Use sensitivity analysis: Try retiring 1–3 years earlier and later. A small change in retirement age often has a large impact on success probability.
    Frequently asked questions▾
    What return rate should I use — 6%, 8%, 10%?

    Use a return rate you believe is realistic for your portfolio mix over the long run. Historical Indian equity returns are around 10–12% nominal, but future returns may be lower. A conservative blended assumption of 8–10% pre-retirement and 7–9% post-retirement is common. The planner's default is 10% pre and 8% post.

    Why is my readiness below 100%? Does that mean I cannot retire?

    Readiness below 100% means your projected corpus is below the deterministic minimum required. It does not mean retirement is impossible — it means the plan is fragile and likely to fail under adverse markets. Use Monte Carlo to see the actual success probability and identify specific actions to close the gap.

    What is a good Monte Carlo success rate?

    There is no universal threshold. Many planners target 80–90% for a moderate risk tolerance and 90–95% for a conservative one. If your success rate is below 70%, the plan is fragile. Below 50%, it is high risk. Consider your ability to reduce expenses, delay retirement, or increase savings before settling on a lower threshold.

    How much should I save each year?

    There is no fixed percentage, but a common starting point is 15–20% of income for Indian professionals in their 30s and 40s. The planner's default of ₹5L on a ₹15L expense base is about 33% of expenses, which is aggressive but achievable for many. Use the recommendations to estimate the exact additional amount needed for your situation.

    Should I include a pension?

    Include it only if you are certain of the amount and timing. Government and corporate pensions are relatively predictable in India, but private pensions vary. The planner assumes pension grows with inflation and is taxed at the rate you enter. If you are unsure, model both with and without pension to see the impact.

    What are LTCG and STCG?

    LTCG = Long-Term Capital Gains (taxed at 12.5% for Indian equities above ₹1.25L). STCG = Short-Term Capital Gains (taxed at 20% for Indian equities). Debt fund gains are taxed as per your income slab after 3 years. The planner blends these based on your equity/debt/arbitrage allocation and equity LTCG/STCG split.

    How does the withdrawal tax work?

    The planner estimates tax each year by blending your asset-class tax rates according to your allocation. It also applies the annual LTCG exemption. This is a simplification — it does not track individual cost basis or tax lots — but it is sufficient for projection purposes.

    Why does the planner show a gap even when I have a large corpus?

    A large corpus can still fall short if expenses are high, inflation is long, returns are lower than expected, or you live longer than planned. The planner compares your corpus against the minimum required to fund your specific expense and longevity assumptions. A gap means the plan is sensitive to bad luck or assumptions.

    Glossary▾
    Corpus
    The total investible wealth available to fund retirement. It grows with returns and contributions, and shrinks with withdrawals and expenses.
    Readiness
    Deterministic coverage ratio: projected corpus at retirement divided by minimum corpus required. 100% means the deterministic projection breaks even.
    Success rate
    In Monte Carlo, the percentage of simulated paths where expenses are fully funded through life expectancy.
    Blended tax rate
    A weighted average of LTCG, STCG, debt, arbitrage, and REIT tax rates based on your portfolio allocation and equity split. REIT taxable gains are taxed at the LTCG rate.
    Annuity due
    A payment stream where cash flows occur at the beginning of each period. In this planner, expenses are treated as an annuity due because they are incurred throughout the year.
    Sequence-of-returns risk
    The danger that poor market returns in the early retirement years permanently reduce a portfolio's ability to recover, even if later returns are strong.
    LTCG exemption
    The annual amount of long-term capital gains that is tax-free. For Indian equities, it is ₹1.25 lakh per year.
    Ad-hoc expense
    A one-time future cost entered by age, such as a house down payment, medical procedure, or child's education.
    Monte Carlo simulation
    A method that runs many random return paths to estimate the range of possible outcomes, rather than a single deterministic path.
    Percentile bands
    In Monte Carlo output, the 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 95th percentile corpus values at each age, showing the spread of outcomes.

    About▾

    Dynamic Retirement Corpus Planner is built to help individuals and families in India plan for a financially secure retirement. It combines deterministic projection with Monte Carlo simulation to answer two core questions:

    • Will my current savings and investments be enough?
    • What is the range of possible outcomes, not just a single estimate?

    The tool models contributions, expenses, inflation, pension income, ad-hoc costs, withdrawal taxes, and market stress scenarios. It is designed for Indian investors and reflects common asset classes, tax rules, and retirement income sources.

    This is a planning and education tool. It does not provide personalised financial, tax, or investment advice. Always review major decisions with a qualified professional.
